CRISPR-SCope Branch Methods: Amplicon Breadth, Robustness, and Editing Rates
============================================================================

Purpose
-------
This note summarizes the amplicon-quality scoring and editing-rate analyses
implemented in this branch. It is intended as a concise methods source for
slides and for reproducing the analyses from CRISPRSCope outputs.

The methods characterize variation among cells within one run. They quantify
the consequences of cell selection, read coverage, and finite cell number;
they do NOT estimate variation across biological replicates, establish a
causal effect of cell-quality selection, or substitute for replicated
experiments.


1. Common inputs, cohorts, and eligibility
-------------------------------------------

The workflow joins two barcode-indexed tables:

* <output_root>.editingSummary.txt: per-cell, per-amplicon first-pass editing
  calls. Each amplicon has a modification percentage (modPct.<amplicon>) and
  a total read count (totCount.<amplicon>).
* <output_root>.amplicon_score.txt: per-barcode supported-breadth score,
  barcode rank, and a cell-quality category (Color).

For every statistical analysis, eligibility is determined separately for each
amplicon. A cell is eligible when BOTH conditions hold:

1. Its first-pass modification percentage for that amplicon is finite (not
   missing or non-numeric).
2. Its total read count for that same amplicon is at least
   min_reads_per_amplicon_per_cell.

The AllCells cohort contains all eligible cells. The InGroup contains eligible
cells whose Color belongs to a category enabled by the include_* settings. If
no include_* setting is supplied, the pipeline defaults to HQ_HI. Every other
eligible cell, including an eligible cell without an amplicon-score row, belongs
to the OutGroup.

Unless otherwise noted, an editing-rate estimate is the arithmetic mean of
the eligible cells' per-cell modification percentages. Eligibility, and hence
the cell count, can differ by amplicon.


2. Supported-amplicon breadth score
-----------------------------------

Goal
~~~~
Classify barcodes using the breadth of amplicon support rather than allowing a
very deeply sequenced amplicon to compensate for missing coverage elsewhere.

Inputs and usable amplicons
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The score begins with first-pass total read counts for every barcode and
amplicon. Amplicon columns containing only missing values are removed. The
remaining columns are usable amplicons. Duplicate amplicon names are rejected.

The existing optional global gate is applied first: a barcode must have at
least min_reads_per_amplicon_per_cell reads at EVERY usable amplicon to be
scored. The default is 0, so this gate is inactive unless configured. After
scoring and ranking, barcodes with fewer than min_total_reads_per_barcode total
reads are removed from the downstream score table (default 10).

Score calculation
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Let U be the number of usable amplicons for a barcode and let S be the number
of those amplicons with at least amplicon_score_min_reads_per_amplicon reads.
Then:

    Amplicon Score = S / U

Each amplicon contributes either zero or one unit of support. Extra reads at a
supported amplicon therefore do not increase the breadth score. The output
also reports S as Supported Amplicons and U as Usable Amplicons.

Barcode ranking and categories
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Barcodes are sorted by total read count, descending. Ties are broken
deterministically by the barcode string in ascending order. The resulting
one-based order is Barcode Rank.

A barcode is high score if:

    S >= ceil(amplicon_score_min_covered_fraction * U)

It is high depth if:

    Barcode Rank <= amplicon_score_max_barcode_rank

The two binary classifications yield four Color codes:

    HQ_HI  high supported breadth, high depth
    HQ_LO  high supported breadth, low depth
    LQ_HI  low supported breadth, high depth
    LQ_LO  low supported breadth, low depth

Default score settings
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
* amplicon_score_min_reads_per_amplicon = 5
* amplicon_score_min_covered_fraction = 2/3 (0.6666666666666666)
* amplicon_score_max_barcode_rank = 10,000
* min_total_reads_per_barcode = 10
* min_reads_per_amplicon_per_cell = 0

Outputs and interpretation
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The score table is written as <output_root>.amplicon_score.txt and is always
regenerated. This prevents a changed scoring setting or scoring algorithm from
silently reusing stale barcode classifications. The score plot displays
barcode rank versus supported-amplicon fraction, with the configured breadth and
rank thresholds marked. The fields are also carried into the .h5ad export.

The score is a coverage-breadth classification, not a measure of editing rate
or a biological cell-state call.


3. Editing-rate point estimates and bootstrap confidence intervals
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Goal
~~~~
Report within-run uncertainty for the mean first-pass editing rate in the
AllCells and InGroup cohorts.

Method
~~~~~~
For each amplicon, calculate the mean modification percentage in AllCells and
in eligible InGroup cells. When a cohort has at least two eligible
cells, repeatedly draw a sample the same size as that cohort WITH REPLACEMENT
from its eligible cells and calculate the sample mean. The lower and upper
limits are the alpha/2 and 1-alpha/2 quantiles of these bootstrap means, where
alpha = 1 - confidence level.

The workflow also produces bootstrap intervals for the InGroup-minus-AllCells
effect. Each bootstrap replicate separately resamples the InGroup and OutGroup
eligible cells, with replacement and at their observed sizes,
then calculates:

    InGroup mean - AllCells mean

The InGroup-minus-OutGroup estimate and interval are also reported. The
latter interval is derived from the paired InGroup-minus-AllCells bootstrap
distribution using the observed cohort sizes.

Defaults
~~~~~~~~
* editing_rate_ci_bootstrap_iterations = 10,000
* editing_rate_ci_confidence_level = 0.95 (central percentile interval)
* editing_rate_ci_seed = 42

Output
~~~~~~
<output_root>.editingRateConfidenceIntervals.txt contains the AllCells,
InGroup, OutGroup, and effect estimates, intervals, eligible cell counts,
resampling settings, and status fields.

Caveat
~~~~~~
These are pointwise bootstrap intervals for the cells in the current run, not
confidence intervals across animals, samples, or other biological replicates.


4. Unconditional InGroup permutation test
-------------------------------------------------

Goal
~~~~
Test whether the observed InGroup editing rate differs from what would be
expected if the same number of eligible cells were assigned to the InGroup at
random.

Method
~~~~~~
For an amplicon with at least two InGroup and two OutGroup eligible cells,
the observed effect is:

    InGroup mean - AllCells mean

For each permutation, InGroup labels are reassigned to a uniformly random
subset of AllCells WITHOUT REPLACEMENT, preserving the observed InGroup size.
The null effect is the permuted InGroup mean minus the fixed AllCells mean.

The two-sided Monte Carlo p-value is:

    (number of |null effect| >= |observed effect| + 1)
    -------------------------------------------------
                 (number of permutations + 1)

Defaults and output
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
* editing_rate_ci_permutation_iterations = 10,000
* The p-value is stored as permutation_p_value in
  <output_root>.editingRateConfidenceIntervals.txt.
* Summary: <output_root>.editingRateUnconditionalPermutation.txt
* Every permuted InGroup mean:
  <output_root>.editingRateUnconditionalPermutationSimulations.txt
* Plot: <output_root>.16_EditingRateUnconditionalPermutation.{png,pdf}

The summary and boxplot use the exact permutation draws used to calculate the
p-value; they are not produced by a second resampling procedure. The plot
includes every estimable amplicon regardless of the coverage-controlled
significance threshold.

This test controls only the InGroup size. It does not control for
per-amplicon read-depth differences between cohorts.


5. Coverage-controlled InGroup-versus-OutGroup test
----------------------------------------------------------

Goal
~~~~
Compare InGroup and OutGroup eligible cells while limiting the comparison
to read-depth ranges represented in both cohorts.

Coverage strata
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Read counts from 0 through editing_rate_ci_coverage_exact_max_reads are exact
strata. Counts above that threshold are placed into consecutive fixed-width
bins. With defaults, counts 0-10 are exact strata and the next bins are 11-15,
16-20, 21-25, and so on.

Only mixed strata--those containing at least one InGroup and one OutGroup
eligible cell--contribute to the controlled effect. A mixed stratum is weighted
by:

    n_InGroup * n_OutGroup / (n_InGroup + n_OutGroup)

The coverage-controlled effect is the weighted mean of the within-stratum
differences:

    InGroup mean - OutGroup mean

The same normalized weights are also applied separately to the stratum-specific
InGroup and OutGroup means. These standardized means are reported as
coverage_standardized_in_group_mean_pct and
coverage_standardized_out_group_mean_pct. Their difference is exactly
coverage_adjusted_in_group_minus_out_group_pct.

Cells in single-cohort strata remain in the unconditional estimates but cannot
contribute to this controlled comparison. The output reports retained cell
counts and percentages, the number of mixed bins, and the remaining weighted
within-bin coverage difference.

Resampling and testing
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Within each mixed stratum, separate InGroup and OutGroup values are
resampled WITH REPLACEMENT at their observed stratum sizes to form a percentile
bootstrap interval for the weighted effect. For the null test, InGroup labels
are reassigned WITHOUT REPLACEMENT only within each mixed stratum while
preserving its observed InGroup count. The same finite-sample-corrected,
two-sided permutation p-value formula is used.

The controlled interval and test require at least two retained cells in each
cohort overall. If there are no mixed strata or too few retained cells, the
corresponding result is marked unavailable rather than imputed.

Defaults and output
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
* editing_rate_ci_coverage_exact_max_reads = 10
* editing_rate_ci_coverage_bin_width_reads = 5
* Uses the bootstrap, permutation, confidence-level, and seed defaults above.
* Results are written to <output_root>.editingRateConfidenceIntervals.txt in
  coverage_standardized_* and coverage_adjusted_* columns.


6. Multiple testing and significance-filtered figures
------------------------------------------------------

The unconditional permutation p-values and the coverage-controlled permutation
p-values are each adjusted separately across all testable amplicons using the
Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate procedure. The resulting columns are
bh_adjusted_p_value and coverage_adjusted_bh_p_value.

An amplicon is considered significant for the standard confidence-interval and
percentage-based depth-stability figures when:

    coverage_adjusted_bh_p_value <= 0.05

The raw-versus-coverage-adjusted comparison figure retains all estimable
amplicons. If no amplicon passes the controlled threshold, the
significance-filtered figures are omitted while the tables and comparison
figure remain available.


7. Percentage-based finite-cohort depth stability
-------------------------------------------------

Goal
~~~~
Measure how estimated editing rates change when only a fraction of the
eligible cells are retained.

Method
~~~~~~
Each amplicon is analyzed independently for both the AllCells and InGroup
cohorts. For a requested percentage p and E eligible cells, the sample size is:

    ceil(E * p / 100), with a minimum of one cell

For each iteration, a random ordering of the eligible cohort is drawn WITHOUT
REPLACEMENT. The first portion of that same ordering supplies each requested
sample size, so the 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 90% samples are nested within an
iteration. The method records the subsample mean, its difference from the full
eligible-cohort mean in percentage points, and the absolute difference.

Across iterations, it reports the median, central percentile interval, median
absolute deviation, and 95th percentile absolute deviation. A deterministic
100% full-cohort reference with zero deviation is appended automatically. A
cohort with fewer than two eligible cells is reported as insufficient rather
than repeatedly sampled.

Defaults and output
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
* editing_rate_depth_stability_iterations = 1,000
* editing_rate_depth_stability_percentages = 10,25,50,75,90
* confidence level = 0.95; seed = 42 (shared with CI settings)
* Table: <output_root>.editingRateDepthStability.txt
* Absolute plot: <output_root>.12_EditingRateDepthStability.{png,pdf}
* Relative plot: <output_root>.13_EditingRateRelativeDepthStability.{png,pdf}

The absolute and relative percentage-based plots are restricted to amplicons
that meet the coverage-controlled BH threshold. Relative deviations are scaled
by each cohort's full editing rate. To avoid unstable ratios near zero, an
amplicon appears in the relative plot only when its full InGroup editing
rate is at least editing_rate_depth_stability_relative_min_hq_edit_pct (default
1%).

Caveat
~~~~~~
This is finite-cohort downsampling, not a confidence interval for new
biological samples. It samples without replacement and therefore differs from
the bootstrap confidence-interval procedure above.


8. Fixed-cell-count finite-cohort depth stability
--------------------------------------------------

Goal
~~~~
Compare stability at identical absolute cell counts across amplicons and
cohorts.

Method
~~~~~~
For each amplicon and for both the AllCells and InGroup cohorts, the workflow
repeats the finite-cohort sampling procedure WITHOUT REPLACEMENT at exactly:

    100, 200, 400, 800, and 1,000 eligible cells

Each replicate produces a mean and its percentage-point deviation from that
cohort's full eligible-cohort mean. The table reports the median estimate,
central interval, median absolute deviation, and 95th percentile absolute
deviation across iterations.

A requested size is used only when the cohort has at least that many eligible
cells. Smaller cohorts are NOT capped to a smaller size and are NOT relabeled;
they receive sample_n_cells = 0, status = insufficient_cells, and missing
subsample statistics for that requested count.

Defaults, output, and caveat
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
* Uses editing_rate_depth_stability_iterations = 1,000,
  editing_rate_ci_confidence_level = 0.95, and editing_rate_ci_seed = 42.
* Table: <output_root>.editingRateFixedCellDepthStability.txt
* Plot: <output_root>.15_EditingRateFixedCellDepthStability.{png,pdf}

Unlike the percentage-based plots, the fixed-count plot includes all amplicons
with available results regardless of editing-rate significance. It remains a
within-run, finite-cohort sensitivity analysis rather than biological
replication.


9. I/O, partial-result, and cache robustness
---------------------------------------------

CRISPResso FASTQ readers inspect the file's gzip magic bytes rather than
assuming compression from its filename. This permits gzip-compressed and plain
text CRISPResso outputs to be read consistently by both the main parser and the
.h5ad loader.

An amplicon that fails or lacks a usable CRISPResso result is retained in the
first-pass editing summary with missing values. It is not converted to an
unedited zero. All-missing amplicons are excluded from the usable-amplicon set
used for supported-breadth scoring, preventing a failed analysis from lowering
every barcode's score.

The amplicon-score table is regenerated on every run. The selected barcode set
used for second-pass filtering is stored as a deterministic SHA-256 digest. If
that set changes, filtered read and allele FASTQs are regenerated; the digest
is retained only after all filtering stages succeed.

For filtered, allele-mode CRISPResso runs, the cache records a SHA-256 digest of
the decompressed allele FASTQ input. A changed or missing input, a previous
failure, or missing completion output invalidates the cached result. Stale
filtered CRISPResso outputs are removed before the affected amplicon is rerun.

Filtered editing summaries use the second-pass filtered CRISPResso
classification while retaining the corresponding original allele-support
counts. Parser caches also record the ignore_substitutions setting so a change
in substitution handling triggers reparsing.


10. Recreating the workflow
----------------------------

1. From first-pass CRISPResso-derived summaries, create a barcode-by-amplicon
   table of total read counts and compute the supported-breadth score and Color
   categories exactly as in Section 2.
2. Join the score table to editingSummary.txt by barcode. For every amplicon,
   construct the eligible AllCells and InGroup cohorts using the two shared
   eligibility rules in Section 1.
3. Calculate AllCells and InGroup means; bootstrap their means and effects with
   replacement for percentile intervals.
4. Generate the unconditional null by repeatedly choosing a same-sized subset
   of eligible cells without replacement. Generate the coverage-controlled
   null by doing the same selection independently within mixed coverage strata.
   Apply BH correction separately to the two families of permutation p-values.
5. Store and summarize the same unconditional permutation draws used for its
   p-value, and plot their distribution with the observed InGroup mean.
6. For depth stability, draw nested percentage samples and fixed-size samples
   without replacement, summarize deviations from each cohort's full mean, and
   preserve unavailable fixed counts as insufficient rather than changing the
   requested size.

Reproducibility notes
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
All resampling is seeded from editing_rate_ci_seed (default 42) with distinct
per-amplicon random streams. Therefore identical input tables and settings
produce the same results in serial and parallel execution. Record the full
settings file, CRISPRSCope version/commit, and generated tables with any figure
used for reporting.
